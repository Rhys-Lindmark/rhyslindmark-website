Why these four buckets

Rhys guessed 25% each. The research disagrees, a little.

BucketClosest predictorSchmidt & Hunter 1998Sackett et al. 2022
Have they done it?Years of experience · job knowledge.18 · .48— · .40
Did they do it?Work sample test (the take-home).54.33
Are they smart?General mental ability.51.31
Everything elseStructured interview.51.42

Numbers are corrected validities: how well each predictor tracks later job performance (0 is a coin flip, 1 is perfect). Sackett, Zhang, Berry & Lievens (2022) found older corrections inflated most of them, and moved the structured interview to first place. Years of experience was weak in 1998 and isn’t in 2022’s top five, so the 2022 preset uses job knowledge (.40) for “have they done it?”. The resume check is the weakest evidence on this page, and the only hard requirement in the job description is one of its rows.

Why not just ask Jev?

The question an applicant tracking system asks: one resume, one job description, one yes or no. Here job is TypeSafe’s Developer Advocate posting, word for word.

The posting says what TypeSafe wants. It doesn’t say how to weigh it. Do five years as a CTO count as “>3 years as a SWE”? Does a YouTube channel about evolution count as a following? Is a missing DevRel title a dealbreaker? One question makes Jev guess all of that at once, so the answer lands near a coin flip. Same mistake as “Should this ticket be escalated?” in Talk 1: the policy is hidden in the model. That’s why this page asks the small judgments above and keeps the hiring policy in code.
