CALIBRATION, FOR EVERYONE IN THE ROOM

A forecaster who says “70% chance of rain” should be right on 70% of those days.

CALIBRATED

When Jev says 0.9, it’s right about 9 times in 10. You can set a threshold and know what you’re buying.

OVERCONFIDENT

Says 0.9, right 6 times in 10. Thresholds lie to you.

UNDERCONFIDENT

Says 0.6, right 9 times in 10. You send too much work to humans.

WHY BUILDERS CARE

Every production Jev story I found has the same shape

p ≥ threshold

Jev handles it

~100 ms, fractions of a cent

ONE QUESTION

How sure is Jev?

p < threshold

A person or a big model

slower, costs more, right more often

Healthcare intake gates, shell-command approval, ticket-fraud screening, eval-every-trace. The threshold is the whole product decision, and it’s only as good as the calibration under it.

THE SKEPTICS ARE RIGHT

“Calibration has to be measured on your dataset.”

Hacker News commenter. Every independent audit I read, from arXiv papers to a Banking77 vs. scikit-learn comparison to vals.ai, reaches the same conclusion: calibration varies by task and wording.

THE BOTTLENECK

Builders have no 20-minute way to check. So skeptics stay skeptical, and believers ship thresholds they never tested.

THE DOCS TODAY

“Plot confidence against accuracy on your data.” True! But no tool, no sample size, no metric, no threshold method.

So on Banking77, Jev isn’t a 1%-error machine. It’s a “handle 72% of traffic at about 5% error, route the rest” machine. That’s a decision you can make, with a chart instead of an argument.

READ THE MISSES BEFORE YOU TRUST ANY NUMBER

Banking77’s most common “error” isn’t Jev’s

MessageHuman labelJev chose
“I do not have my pin”get_physical_cardpasscode_forgotten
“Cannot access my top up.”topping_up_by_cardpending_top_up
“What should I do with my atm that got stuck?”card_swallowedatm_support

get_physical_card → change_pin was the top confusion (6 of 70 misses). Some misses are label noise, some are near-duplicate intents, and a few are Jev. Trust Meter lists the most confident misses so a person reads them first.

WHAT BUILDING THIS TAUGHT ME ABOUT JEV

A probability is an interface between a model and your code.
The threshold is policy, and policy lives in code.

Jev’s job is to make the number honest. Your job is to measure it on your data and decide what it’s worth.
