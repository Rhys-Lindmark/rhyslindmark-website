WHAT BUILDERS SAY

“I have tested jev for my use cases and its horrendously wrong… I’ll pass for now.”

Hacker News, launch week. The reply from the team: “you need to boil the question down further.”

This talk is about what “boil the question down” actually means, and why it’s the main thing standing between people and their first Jev build.

JEV IN ONE SLIDE

A model that answers typed questions about your data, with probabilities your code can use

// state: what a person would look at
{ "message": "Charged twice for March, refund the duplicate?" }

// questions: small, typed
refund: { type: "noul",
  instructions: "Is the sender asking for money back?" }

refund → { noul: 0.99 }
topic  → { choice: "billing", p: 1.0 }

201 ms · 416 tokens · $0.00002

Four question types: Noul (yes/no), Choice, Score, Box. No free text, so nothing your code can choke on.

WHAT BUILDING THIS TAUGHT ME ABOUT JEV’S PHILOSOPHY

SYSTEM TWO · THE LLM

Thinks.

Plans, writes, reasons for seconds.

SYSTEM ONE · JEV

Notices.

Snap judgments, ~100 ms, with probabilities.

CODE

Decides.

Policy, math, thresholds: readable and testable.

The mistake is asking any one layer to do another’s job.

HOW I KNOW THIS IS THE BOTTLENECK

I read what builders say. Then I had Jev read it, and it corrected me.

THE CORPUS

~60k

public posts, issues and repos about Jev: GitHub, Hacker News, X, the web. Refreshed every morning.

JEV READS ITS REVIEWS

11¢

2,324 posts × 12 judgments each. My keyword count said people love typed answers. Jev said speed is what they praise.

THE TELL

8

posts out of 2,324 name “question design”. The top GitHub pain is “it got my task wrong”. The fix is the question; people don’t know that’s the problem.

Nobody names it, so it isn’t a bug report. It’s a teaching job, which is where developer relations has the most leverage.

THE CHECKLIST

Five checks before you send a question

1 · The human test

Could a smart person, shown only this state, answer at a glance?

2 · Judgment, not decision

“Is production down?” not “Should we escalate?”

3 · Beware the “or”

“Rude or drunk?” is two Nouls. Combine them in code.

4 · Ask about nothing

A Choice always picks. Pair it with a Noul: “does anything fit?”

5 · Math goes in code

Extract the date with Jev; compare it in code. Jev isn’t a calculator.

Then name the confusing case

In the criteria: “a SOC 2 question is not an incident.”

THE BOTTLENECK

Jev moves the hard part from writing prompts to designing questions. Nobody teaches that yet.

FROM HACKER NEWS

“There is no way to mark absentia.” Fix: always give an explicit option for the “none of the above” cases.

FROM GITHUB ISSUES

An empty STATE: header changes Choice answers. Single-select Choice forced onto multi-label jobs.

FROM PRACTITIONERS

The best builders ask several yes/no questions and feed the probabilities into a logistic regression.

Prompt skills don’t transfer. The unit of work is no longer “write instructions.” It’s “design a question a smart person could answer from this state alone.”

CALIBRATION, FOR EVERYONE IN THE ROOM

A forecaster who says “70% chance of rain” should be right on 70% of those days.

CALIBRATED

When Jev says 0.9, it’s right about 9 times in 10. You can set a threshold and know what you’re buying.

OVERCONFIDENT

Says 0.9, right 6 times in 10. Thresholds lie to you.

UNDERCONFIDENT

Says 0.6, right 9 times in 10. You send too much work to humans.

WHEN JEV SAYS p, IS IT RIGHT p OF THE TIME?

what Jev said → (0 to 1)

274 of 400 answers came in at ~0.98 and were right 96% of the time.

In the middle, Jev is overconfident: answers at ~0.54 were right 35% of the time. Those are the confusable intents (card_arrival vs. card_delivery_estimate).

“SO WHAT IS JEV FOR?”

Anywhere your code needs a judgment a person makes in under a second

Today you useWhere it breaksJev instead
Regex, keyword listsMeaning hides behind wording: cat ~/.sshTyped judgments about meaning
A trained classifierNeeds thousands of labels; a new label means retrainingZero-shot, then calibrate on 50–200 rows
An LLM + JSON parsing~1 s, cost per call, unparseable replies, policy buried in a prompt~80 ms, typed output, policy in code
JevArithmetic, dates, long reasoning, generating textDo those in code, or hand off to an LLM

WHAT BUILDING THIS TAUGHT ME ABOUT JEV

A probability is an interface between a model and your code.
The threshold is policy, and policy lives in code.

Jev’s job is to make the number honest. Your job is to measure it on your data and decide what it’s worth.

WHAT TO TAKE HOME

IF YOU’VE HEARD THE HYPE

Jev answers what a smart person could answer at a glance.

Your job is to ask that question. The rest is ordinary code you can read.

IF YOU’RE ABOUT TO BUILD

Write the truth table first.

List the inputs it needs. The ones that need language understanding are your questions. Lint them. Policy, math and dates stay in code.

WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE AS THE JOB

Make the skill obvious and the proof cheap, where builders and their agents already look.

DAYS 1–30 · FIX THE DEFAULTS

The 200-word preamble in llms.txt and SDK docstrings (agents’ decision-shaped integrations: 25% → 0%). How to Ask Jev next to Primitives. Trust Meter as a cookbook. A weekly “what builders hit” memo to product.

DAYS 31–60 · SHOW IT IN PUBLIC

Three talks, one per bottleneck, each with a 10-minute cut. “Replace one LLM call a week”, live, with a real upstream PR. The game as the on-ramp for the hype crowd.

DAYS 61–90 · MAKE IT A COMMUNITY

A hackathon kit judged on question design and honest calibration. A prize for the most instructive miss. Bots that spot stuck builders and draft help, never spam.
