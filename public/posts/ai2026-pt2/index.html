OpenAI and Anthropic are massive. They have over $100 billion of combined revenue.

They’re the fastest-growing companies we’ve ever seen.

There is a massive difference vs. Nvidia though — Ant & OAI have terrible margins.

OpenAI and Ant both spend $60 on COGS (inference) to make $100 of revenue.

But for opex (training R&D), OpenAI spends ~$160 vs. Anthropic’s $40.

This chart breaks down Anthropic’s business.

OpenAI doesn’t seem to have the same margins yet.

We’ll see if OpenAI can run the margin expansion playbook that Amazon, Uber, and Anthropic have run. They have a lot of ground to cover:

In addition to compute, there’s massive pressure on another leg of the intelligence triangle…

Data

Data is the fuel for compute. ↗

The first LLMs used the most easily accessible data, the human internet slop. I love Karpathy’s framing here: early models compressed the 100 TB internet into 100 GB of parameters. Learning is compression.

After pretraining, LLMs are a lossy JPG of the internet; a document creator doing next-token prediction. After some chat RLHF, it nudges them to become good at chatting. And voilà, ChatGPT.

It took work to ingest the internet, but models were still dumb. Or at least their intelligence was spiky:

MMLU was the first big benchmark. It’s a hard test. Specific domain experts only get 90% in their target field. 175B param GPT-3 got 40%. 1.8T param MoE GPT-4 got 80%. It’s now been completely saturated. GPQA (PhD-level questions) quickly became completely saturated too. Humanity’s Last Exam is the current frontier.

To beat all of these benchmarks, we needed more data (ofc). But also a new training paradigm, Chain of Thought. Thinking step by step. I love Bob McGrew’s framing here:

“GPT 3, 4, 5 use 100x more compute each generation for training.
o1 is packing 10,000x more compute into an answer.”

Fundamentally, o1 didn’t need new data from humans. No new internet-sized reasoning library. Instead, o1 needed PhD’s (from Mercor et al) to create difficult tasks and judging criteria. Verifiable rewards in domains like math and coding (RLVR). Then o1 sculpts its own reasoning library through millions of reasoning trajectories. And boom, the model learns to think better thoughts. RL is bitter lesson pilled.

But we don’t just want the AIs to reason. It’s not enough to solve math problems.

Read Levent Alpöge’s post

We want AIs to do useful work. To be agents. To add to GDP.

Mostly this means being able to do lengthy tasks. Not just chat. Not just reason. But creatively pursue a goal.

This is the cutting edge of data. Agentic trajectories, not just reasoning trajectories. More than anything, agentic trajectories need an agentic harness. They need a while loop:

while (task != complete):
    use tools to complete task

With MCP, grep (read), and str_replace_editor (write), LLMs became agents. Like Charmeleon becoming Charizard. Then give them a gym of tens of thousands of Mac Minis to RL on computer use:

The data frontier has shifted from learning human representations to learning model trajectories.

Which has shifted the compute spend of training large models:

I love how Jie Tang of Z.ai talks about it—we’ve moved from scaling pretraining compute to RL compute:

Read Jie Tang’s post

Total parameters appear to matter up to a threshold — enough to hold the world — after which additional capability comes from scaling elsewhere: effective depth per forward pass, and above all post-training.

GLM-5.3 is our controlled experiment on that claim. Same base, same architecture, same total and activated parameters as GLM-5.2.

One month of scaling long-horizon environments and RL. The gains are not marginal. Well, scaling has more than one dial. We turned the post-training one this time because it had the most slack left in it — not because the others are finished.

Jie Tang, Z.ai

We pumped those FLOPs and parameters until we couldn’t anymore. Up to 1e26 and 1T parameters.

Now, as Jie says, we can push on RL. To get ~20T tokens of RL rollout trajectories. The chart below has same x and y axes, but now adds RL rollouts as bubble size. You can see GLM 5.2 mostly gets bigger in terms of RL, not parameters!

Or you can view it as trajectory depth. These days you’ll have ~100 average tool steps in the RL training curriculum. While GPT-3 had ~none.

All of this data and RL has helped us move from preschoolers on a GPU (GPT-2) to geniuses in a data center (Opus 4.5) to agents on a mission (Fable 5.1, GPT 5.6).

The marginal cost of coding has dropped precipitously. (I have 5+ agents coding random ideas I have, spending over a billion tokens a day.)

Cheap coding (and other cognitive labor) is a very useful service. OpenAI and Anthropic make over ~$50B/yr in revenue providing it. Even though their margins are small or negative, Chinese open-weight models are still trying to commoditize the model layer. They’re beginning to succeed.

Models

For a while now, Chinese models have lagged behind US models by 7 months:

Epoch AI. It’s kept this trend into Sep 2026.

But the models generally have become wicked smaht. So being frontier doesn’t matter quite as much. US firms have shifted to Chinese models for their tokens:

Of course, OpenRouter isn’t all tokens. It’s likely less than 10%. They’re in the tens of trillions, while OAI and Ant are in the hundreds of trillions.

But this is impressive. Especially because DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM were trained on only 1e25 FLOPs, while US foundation models use 1e26 or 1e27.

By building in the open, Chinese models can learn more from each other:

Read Elie Bakouch’s post

I’m not sure if open-weight models will impact closed-weight models’ ARR. I think Ant & OAI have super strong flywheels of data, talent, and cost of capital. Perhaps most importantly, they have a great brand. Aggregation Theory still matters, even in the age of AI.

But I want to look now at the top of the AI stack, the app layer. With commoditized compute and tokens, what apps can we build?
