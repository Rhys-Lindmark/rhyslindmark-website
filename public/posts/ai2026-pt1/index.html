RHYSAI 2026 / PART 0101 / 39

The data center buildout is now 2% of GDP, reaching the size of the railroads, cars, highways, and telecoms.

We build more data centers than offices.

These complexes are massive.

Nvidia is at the center of the data center buildout. Chip production has 10x’ed from 2M H100e to over 20M H100e.

This has increased their revenue from $100B to $300B a year.

Nvidia is making over 50% of all company’s revenues from GenAI.

Plus, they’re doing so with never-before-seen operating margins. They have 66% operating margins compared to 33% for GAFA.

And an annualized profit of $250B compared to $125B for GAFA.

This is even more impressive when you compare it to previous eras. IBM, Wintel, and GAFA had around 25% margin. Nvidia has averaged 56%.

Nvidia is a really good business. They’ve become the most valuable company in the world.

Now everyone else wants part of the GPU pie. Nvidia’s margin is the rest of the industry’s opportunity.

The 100 GW Opportunity

…So what’s the actual opportunity here? Has Nvidia already eaten all the profits? Likely not.

From 2020 to 2025, we built 10 GW+ of data center capacity. Nvidia GPUs filled 70% of them.

But we’re likely to 10x to 100 GW by 2040.

...It’s easy to add a bar to a chart.

It’s hard to actually build a gigawatt.

A gigawatt is massive.

It has giga in the name.

It’s 1,000,000 US homes. The electricity for a whole city.

It’s tough to know how many data centers will actually get built and wired up.

Epoch estimates 100 GW by 2030 (not 2040)

But their data center permit database only shows 32 GW by 2028.

Part of this is a permitting issue. By 2028, a16z net new estimates are: 44 GW planned, 25 GW permitted, 19 GW shortfall.

Part of this is an electricity issue. The US is at 1 TW trying to get to 2 TW. While China is at 4 TW, planning to get to 8 TW.

In any scenario, expect 100 GW of data centers soon, mostly in the US and China.

All of these data centers will need GPUs.

Thus far, Nvidia has kept its market share as the main provider of GPUs—around 70% of a growing pie.

But going forward, this pie is up for grabs.

Each GW powers ~$40B of chips. As we get more efficient, each GW will power ~$4B.

So roughly the opportunity here is: 100 GW * $20B/GW = $2T opportunity to build chips.

Many companies are trying to take Nvidia’s margin:

1. Hyperscalers like Amazon Trainium and Google TPUs.

The Trainium 3 might become the Amazon Basics of GPUs. Anthropic committed to 5 GW of compute through AWS. Lots will be Nvidia chips (see new 2M GPU Amazon-Nvidia commitment), but lots will be Trainium chips.

Google might be getting out of the foundation model game to focus entirely on GCP (Gemini is Cooked but GCP is Cooking). They’re expanding their TPU lineup into training (8t) vs. inference (8i).

Roughly speaking, I see Trainium and TPUs as fighting for the training, not inference, market.

2. Startups like Cerebras (~$44B market cap), Etched ($21B valuation), and Samba Nova ($11B valuation).

Most of these (decacorn-sized) startups are focused on inference.

Cerebras is going to add 750MW of ultra-low latency chips to power ChatGPT’s new Ultrafast mode. On Ultrafast, Sol 5.6 answered 2,500 HLE questions in 11 hours vs. Claude Fable 5 in 78 hours. In other words, “Ultrafast worked through the frontier of human knowledge in a single working day.” (!!!)

Etched is building frontier inference clusters that break up then optimize pre-fill and decode. Pre-fill is compute-constrained, so they’re compute maxxing by voltage minimizing (LVI). Decode is memory-constrained, so they’re building Cluster Scale Memory with a better NVLink, so larger clusters can decode at once. As their founders said recently on Invest Like The Best, it’s crazy that training clusters like Colossus are 100,000 chips networked together, while inference clusters are still just eight chips.

3. Model providers like OpenAI.

OAI released Jalapeño’s first results at the end of August. It’s also focused on inference.

And damn, it’s impressive. It’s Pareto-dominant against GB200s and VR200s at tokens/s and tok/s/MW:

OpenAI bought 40% of all DRAM. Then used their own models for chip design. There are a few great articles analyzing their design (1, 2). Fundamentally, I understand it as a chip that is not optimized for training, nor even optimized for inference generally. It’s a decode-first, watt-minimizing chip, optimized for efficiently streaming 1 TB of model weights out of HBM.
